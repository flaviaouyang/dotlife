# Apple Health

Apple Health is the Health app on iPhone. It stores data from the iPhone itself, a paired Apple Watch and any app or device the person lets write to it. A full export can fill `activity`, `workouts`, `sleep`, `heart`, `body`, `fitness_markers`, `vitals`, `nutrition`, `mindfulness`, `cycle`, `symptoms` and `glucose`. `medication` and `mood` are only partly covered (see below), and Screen Time is not in Health.

`cycle`, `medication`, `symptoms` and `glucose` are sensitive categories. Switch one on in `config.toml` only after the person agrees.

## What it fills

Type names are the `type` attribute of a `Record` in `export.xml`. They match the HealthKit identifiers ([`HKQuantityTypeIdentifier`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier), [`HKCategoryTypeIdentifier`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier)) with an `HKQuantityTypeIdentifier` or `HKCategoryTypeIdentifier` prefix. Always convert from the record's `unit` attribute, which can be in the person's display units (see "Watch out for").

## Getting the data out

HealthKit has no web or cloud API. "The user's device stores all HealthKit data locally", and the store is encrypted while the device is locked ([Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)). iPhone, Apple Watch, Vision Pro and iPad (iPadOS 17 and later) each keep their own store and sync it; a Mac has no store of its own ([About the HealthKit framework](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/about-the-healthkit-framework)). Every way out therefore starts on the iPhone.

### 1. Export All Health Data (best for a script)

Steps, from Apple's iPhone User Guide ([Share your data in Health on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/share-your-health-data-iph5ede58c3d/ios)):

1. Open the Health app.
2. Tap Summary, then tap your picture or initials.
3. Tap Export All Health Data, then choose a way to share it (e.g. Save to Files, AirDrop to a Mac).

Apple says only that it exports "all of your health and fitness data" in XML. The rest of this section comes from community sources, because Apple publishes no schema for the file:

- The result is `export.zip`. Unzipped, it holds a folder (`apple_health_export`) with `export.xml`, `export_cda.xml` (a Clinical Document Architecture copy, not needed here), a `workout-routes` folder of GPX files and, if the person has ECGs, an `electrocardiograms` folder ([Apple Community, 2026](https://communities.apple.com/de/thread/256220417); [healthkit-to-sqlite](https://github.com/dogsheep/healthkit-to-sqlite/blob/main/healthkit_to_sqlite/utils.py)).
- `export.xml` starts with an inline DTD that names its version (`<!-- HealthKit Export Version: 13 -->` in a 2024 export) ([example export](https://github.com/AbhikChowdhury6/dataImport/blob/main/appleHK/exampleExportBits.xml)). The root `HealthData` holds `ExportDate`, `Me`, then `Record`, `Correlation`, `Workout`, `ActivitySummary` and `ClinicalRecord` elements.
- `Record` attributes: `type`, `unit`, `value`, `sourceName`, `sourceVersion`, `device`, `creationDate`, `startDate`, `endDate`, with optional `MetadataEntry key value` children ([healthkit11.dtd](https://github.com/AlekSi/applehealth/blob/main/healthkit11.dtd)). Quantity records carry a number in `value`; category records carry the value name, e.g. `value="HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysisAsleepREM"`.
- `Workout` attributes: `workoutActivityType` (e.g. `HKWorkoutActivityTypeRunning`), `duration`, `durationUnit`, `totalDistance`, `totalDistanceUnit`, `totalEnergyBurned`, `totalEnergyBurnedUnit`, `sourceName`, `sourceVersion`, `device`, `creationDate`, `startDate`, `endDate`. Children: `MetadataEntry`, `WorkoutEvent`, `WorkoutRoute` (with a `FileReference path="/workout-routes/....gpx"`) and, in version 13, `WorkoutStatistics` with `type`, `startDate`, `endDate`, `average`, `minimum`, `maximum`, `sum`, `unit` ([example export](https://github.com/AbhikChowdhury6/dataImport/blob/main/appleHK/exampleExportBits.xml)). Apple has deprecated `HKWorkout.totalDistance` and `totalEnergyBurned` in favour of per-type statistics ([HKWorkout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkout)), so prefer `WorkoutStatistics` when present.
- `ActivitySummary` has one row per day: `dateComponents`, `activeEnergyBurned`, `appleExerciseTime`, `appleStandHours` and their goals.
- Dates look like `2026-03-02 07:00:00 +0100`: local time with the offset ([TDDA](https://www.tdda.info/in-defence-of-xml-exporting-and-analysing-apple-health-data)).
- The `device` attribute is a printed object, e.g. `<<HKDevice: 0x...>, name:Apple Watch, manufacturer:Apple Inc., model:Watch, hardware:Watch6,1, software:7.6>` ([example export](https://github.com/AbhikChowdhury6/dataImport/blob/main/appleHK/exampleExportBits.xml)). Its fields match [`HKDevice`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkdevice) (name, manufacturer, model, hardware and software version). `sourceName` is the name of the app or device that wrote the sample ([HKSource](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hksource)), often the person's own device name, e.g. "Alice's Apple Watch".

Parsing: `export.xml` is large (one person's 5.5 MB zip expanded to 109 MB in 2016, [TDDA](https://www.tdda.info/in-defence-of-xml-exporting-and-analysing-apple-health-data); years of watch data are far larger). Stream it: in Python, `xml.etree.ElementTree.iterparse` (or `XMLPullParser`) on `start` and `end` events, because the tree is "not freed incrementally" otherwise ([Python docs](https://docs.python.org/3/library/xml.etree.elementtree.html)). Track the depth: handle a `Record` or `Workout` only when it is a direct child of `HealthData` (records inside a `Correlation` repeat top-level ones, see "Watch out for"), then clear the root (`root.clear()`, with `root` taken from the first `start` event), since clearing only the element leaves the root holding every emptied child. Read `export.xml` straight from the zip without extracting it. Group records by `type` and keep only the types in the table above.

What it needs: an iPhone with Health. Limits: it is manual and all-or-nothing (no date range), so each sync means a new full export. A script should re-read the whole file and stage every row again, with no window.

### 2. A HealthKit export app (for scheduled exports)

Only an app on the iPhone can read HealthKit. Third-party apps exist that export to files on a schedule, for example [Health Auto Export](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1115567069) (CSV, JSON; automatic exports to iCloud Drive, Dropbox, a REST API and others in its paid tier). Its help says it also exports State of Mind (iOS 18 and later) and Medications (iOS 26 and later) ([Health Auto Export help](https://help.healthyapps.dev/en/health-auto-export/manual-export/)). This is an option, not a recommendation: it is a third party, and its output format is its own, so map from its docs, not from this guide. Name it in `sources.md` before sending data through it.

### 3. Shortcuts

The Shortcuts app has a Find Health Samples action, which can return sleep phases (core, deep, REM) since iOS 16.2 ([What's new in Shortcuts](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101583)). A shortcut can run on a Time of Day personal automation ([Create a personal automation](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/create-a-new-personal-automation-apdfbdbd7123/ios)) and save a file to iCloud Drive. This is fiddly to build per data type, and an app may not be able to read HealthKit while the phone is locked ([Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)). Use it only if the person already likes Shortcuts.

### 4. An AI app connector

The Claude iOS app can read Apple Health (activity, workouts, vitals, body measurements, sleep, nutrition), in beta, US only, Pro and Max plans, read-only ([Use Claude with iOS apps](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11869619-use-claude-with-ios-apps)). That fits answering questions, not filling files: values would be transcribed by hand from a chat. Prefer the export.

## Choices to make

Settle these with the person before writing the script, and write the answers in `sources.md` under an "Apple Health" heading. The ones the script reads go under `[apple-health]` in `config.toml`, e.g. `export = "inbox/export.zip"`, `sleep_gap_min = 60` and `apps = { "Health Mate" = "withings" }` (each app's `sourceName` and the `source` value written for it).

- **Where the export goes.** `inbox/export.zip` in the repo by default; git ignores `inbox/`. For another place (e.g. an iCloud Drive folder), set `export = "<path>"` under `[apple-health]` in `config.toml`. Each time they want fresh data, they export again and replace the file.
- **Which device wins**, for every category the watch and the phone both write to: steps, distance and floors, but also sleep (the phone writes an in-bed sample each night) and the others. The usual answer: the watch, and the phone only on a date the watch has nothing for that category (see below). A row names one device, so the choice is per date and category, not per column.
- **Other apps' data.** Health also holds what other apps write to it, such as a smart scale's app writing weight. Decide per type whether to use it and which wins, e.g. the scale over weight typed into Health. This ranking happens inside the Apple Health script, because to dotlife it is all one source, `apple-health`.
- **The sleep gap.** How long a break between sleep samples can be and still count as one night. An hour suits most people.
- **Symptom severities**, if they track symptoms: the number on the 0 to 10 scale for each of Health's levels.
- **How far back.** Every year in the export, or from a date they choose. The script reads the whole file either way.

## Mapping to the format

- **Time zone and dates.** Parse `startDate` and `endDate` with their offset, convert to the `timezone` in `config.toml`, and take the local date. Write timestamps as ISO 8601 with the offset, e.g. `2026-03-02T07:00+01:00`.
- **Durations across a clock change.** Work out lengths and gaps (sleep minutes, mindful minutes, the sleep gap) from the parsed times or in UTC, never after converting both ends to the local zone: Python subtracts two times in the same `ZoneInfo` by wall clock, so a sample from 02:53 +02:00 to 03:39 +01:00 would come out as 46 minutes instead of 106. Convert only to find the date and to write timestamps.
- **Daily sums** (steps, exercise minutes, energy, distance, floors, nutrition, mindful minutes): filter to one source, then sum samples by the local date of `startDate`. A sample that crosses midnight goes wholly to its start date; that is close enough for minute-length samples.
- **Daily averages** (HRV, SpO2, respiratory rate, blood pressure, glucose, weight): average the day's samples from one source.
- **Picking a source.** Decide per type which source wins and write it in `sources.md`. Identify sources by the `device` model (`model:Watch`, `model:iPhone`) or by `sourceName`, not by summing. Suggested `source` values: `apple-watch`, `iphone`, `entered-in-health` (typed into Health by hand), or the writing app's name in lowercase (e.g. `withings`) when a third-party app wrote it. Don't copy `sourceName` itself into the file: it often contains the person's name. Avoid `manual` here: in dotlife, `manual` is the source for rows the person adds to `data/sources/manual/` themselves.
- **Typed-in values.** HealthKit marks a sample entered by the user with the metadata key [`HKMetadataKeyWasUserEntered`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkmetadatakeywasuserentered) set to true. Unverified: the export's spelling of the key (community parsers show `HKWasUserEntered` with value `1`). A typed-in value can also show as `sourceName` "Health" with no `device`. Check the person's own export before relying on either.
- **Steps and distance.** The watch is usually more complete on days it is worn. A simple rule: use the watch; on a day with no watch samples at all, use the iPhone and write `iphone` in `source`. Never add the two.
- **Workouts.** One row per `Workout`. `date` is the local date of `startDate`. `type` from `workoutActivityType` with the `HKWorkoutActivityType` prefix removed and lowercased (`Running` to `running`); keep the same words over time. `avg_hr_bpm`, `distance_m` and `energy_kcal` from `WorkoutStatistics` as in the table; leave empty when absent.
- **Sleep sessions.** The export has no session id. Take sleep samples of one source, sort by start, and join samples whose gap is under a threshold you choose (an hour works for most people; note it in `sources.md`). A session's `start` is its first sample and `end` its last; `date` is the local date of `end` (the wake date). `inBed` samples overlap the stage samples ([HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis)), so use them only for `start` and `end` when there are no stage samples, never in a minute sum. The stage values exist only from iOS 16 and watchOS 9 ([asleepCore](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis/asleepcore)); older samples use the deprecated `asleep` value ([HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis)), so count it in `asleep_min` and leave the stage columns empty for those nights. A session with only `inBed` samples (e.g. from iPhone bedtime) has empty sleep minutes.
- **Resting heart rate.** The system may replace earlier samples for the current or previous day with better estimates ([restingHeartRate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/restingheartrate)). Use the last sample of each day, and re-sync the last two days each time.
- **HRV.** Use RMSSD samples on days that have them. On days with only SDNN (before iOS 27, or a device that writes only SDNN), use SDNN, and say in `sources.md` that `hrv_ms` is SDNN up to that date. SDNN and RMSSD are different measures, so expect a step in the chart where it switches; never convert one into the other.
- **VO2 max.** The watch writes a sample only after an Outdoor Walk, Outdoor Run or Hiking workout on fairly flat ground with good GPS and heart rate, not on the first workout, and estimates between 14 and 60 ml/kg/min per the developer docs ([vo2Max](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/vo2max)); Apple Support gives 14 to 65 ([Track your cardio fitness levels](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108790)). Indoor workouts don't count. Write a row only on days with a new `HKQuantityTypeIdentifierVO2Max` record; with several, keep the last. Match the unit by meaning: the export may write it as `mL/min·kg` rather than `ml/kg/min`. FORMAT.md keeps only estimates from the person's own workouts. A sample's [`HKMetadataKeyVO2MaxTestType`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkmetadatakeyvo2maxtesttype) says how it was made, one of [`HKVO2MaxTestType`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkvo2maxtesttype) `maxExercise`, `predictionSubMaxExercise`, `predictionNonExercise` ("without any exercise") or `predictionStepTest`; skip `predictionNonExercise`. Unverified: the number the export writes for each (Apple's page doesn't list the raw values), so check the person's records, and ask them when a sample has no test type.
- **Symptoms.** One row per day and symptom. The symptom name is the type without `HKCategoryTypeIdentifier`, in lowercase words (`headache`, `lower back pain`). Health's severity has named levels, not a 0 to 10 number. Agree a number for each level with the person before writing any (for example `NotPresent` 0, `Mild`, `Moderate` and `Severe` on the 0 to 10 scale) and record the mapping in `sources.md`. For `Unspecified` and for presence-only types, write the row with `severity` empty: the symptom was present at an unrecorded severity. Several entries on one day: keep the highest.
- **Cycle.** `flow_level` per day from `HKCategoryTypeIdentifierMenstrualFlow`; with several samples on a day, keep the highest level. A day with only `Unspecified` samples gets a row with `flow_level` empty, so it still counts as a period day.
- **Zero or empty.** A value is 0 only when the source measured nothing; otherwise the cell is empty:
  - Sleep stages: on a night with stage samples, a stage with none is 0 minutes; on a night without stage samples, the stage columns are empty.
  - Exercise minutes: take the day's `ActivitySummary` `appleExerciseTime` when there is one, which is 0 on a day the watch was worn without exercise; with no summary and no samples, leave it empty.
  - Energy: write `energy_kcal` only on a day with both active and resting energy; with one of them, leave it empty rather than write half the day.
  - Floors, distance, nutrition and mindful minutes: empty on a day without samples.
- **Rounding.** Sums of floats pick up long tails (4132.999 m). Round counts, minutes, metres and kcal to whole numbers, weight to 0.01 kg, and heart rate variability, VO2 max and temperatures to one decimal.
- **Leave empty** what Health doesn't have for that day. Don't fill from `ActivitySummary` goals or from another day.

A made-up example row for Alice:

```csv
date,source,steps,active_min,energy_kcal,distance_m,floors
2026-03-02,apple-watch,9120,41,2210,6830,7
```

## Watch out for

- **Double-counted steps, distance and floors.** Apple Watch and iPhone both record them ([stepCount](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/stepcount)), and the export keeps every source's samples. The Health app's own totals use a source priority list (Browse, a category, Data Sources & Access, Edit) ([Manage Health data](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108779)), but that priority isn't applied to the export. Filter by source yourself.
- **Display units.** Records carry the unit they were written in, which can be the person's preferred unit, e.g. `lb` for weight and `ft` for height in a real export ([example export](https://github.com/AbhikChowdhury6/dataImport/blob/main/appleHK/exampleExportBits.xml)). Glucose is mg/dL or mmol/L by region ([bloodGlucose](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/bloodglucose)). Convert every record from its own `unit`. HealthKit's mole unit for glucose carries the molar mass, `mol<180.15588000005408>` ([HKUnit init(from:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkunit/init(from:)-9qont)), so expect a mmol/L unit string to include it; match on the prefix, not the exact string.
- **Percentages are fractions.** Body fat and SpO2 are 0.0 to 1.0 ([HKUnit percent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkunit/percent())).
- **Correlations repeat records.** Blood pressure (and food) records inside a `Correlation` also appear as top-level `Record`s ([healthkit11.dtd](https://github.com/AlekSi/applehealth/blob/main/healthkit11.dtd)). Read top-level records only.
- **Repeated or revised values.** VO2 max is only written after qualifying outdoor workouts, so it can sit unchanged for months; don't carry it forward. Health can also offer to update past cardio fitness estimates ([Track your cardio fitness levels](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108790)), so re-read the whole export each time rather than appending.
- **Travel.** Sleep samples may carry an `HKMetadataKeyTimeZone` metadata entry, which Apple recommends for sleep ([sleepAnalysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier/sleepanalysis), [HKMetadataKeyTimeZone](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkmetadatakeytimezone)). dotlife's dates follow `config.toml`, not the zone the person was in.
- **Wrist temperature.** The export holds the absolute nightly value, which goes in `skin_temp_c`. Health shows it relative to a baseline it needs about five nights to build ([appleSleepingWristTemperature](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/applesleepingwristtemperature)), but that baseline isn't in the export, so leave `skin_temp_delta_c` empty rather than working out a baseline yourself. Apple names Apple Watch Series 8 and Apple Watch Ultra as able to record it, with Sleep Focus on and the watch worn overnight.
- **Not in the export.** State of Mind entries were missing from the XML export in December 2024 and January 2025 reports ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255896268)); no later confirmation either way was found. Medication dose events (iOS 26) are not confirmed in the export either. For both, check the person's own `export.xml` for the types before planning on them.
- **Broken exports.** Version 12 exports from iOS 16.0 and 16.1 had an invalid DTD and duplicate `startDate` attributes, fixed in iOS 16.2 ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254202523)). If a parser fails, re-export on a current iOS.
- **Size and time.** Exports of several years of watch data are large and take minutes to build on the phone. Never commit `export.zip` or `export.xml`; keep them outside the repo or in an ignored folder.
- **Screen Time** is not Health data: HealthKit has no screen-time type ([HKQuantityTypeIdentifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier), [HKCategoryTypeIdentifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier)). `screen_time` needs another source.

## Not in the format

- Heart rate samples during the day (`HKQuantityTypeIdentifierHeartRate`) and walking heart rate average.
- Heart rate recovery one minute after exercise (`HKQuantityTypeIdentifierHeartRateRecoveryOneMinute`).
- Stand time and stand hours (`HKQuantityTypeIdentifierAppleStandTime`, `ActivitySummary` `appleStandHours`).
- Time in daylight (`HKQuantityTypeIdentifierTimeInDaylight`).
- Sleeping breathing disturbances (`HKQuantityTypeIdentifierAppleSleepingBreathingDisturbances`).
- Walking metrics: walking speed, step length, asymmetry, double support, walking steadiness.
- Audio exposure (environmental and headphone).
- Body mass index, lean body mass.
- Micronutrients, sugar, fibre, sodium.
- Cycling, swimming and other distances outside workouts.
- Activity ring goals from `ActivitySummary`.
- Workout routes (GPX), ECG recordings, audiograms, clinical records.
- State of Mind valence (a number from an app that reads HealthKit), labels and associations.
